## 🧪 The Layer Test
The Layer Test is a confirmatory qualitative test used specifically to distinguish 
between  $(Br^-)$ and $(I^-)$ ions in an organic compound's sodium fusion extract.
### The Chemistry Behind the Test
The test operates on two fundamental chemical principles:

* `Oxidative Displacement`: Chlorine $(\mathrm{Cl}_2)$ is more electronegative and a 
stronger oxidizing agent than Bromine or Iodine. It displaces these halogens 
from their salt solutions.

* `Differential Solubility`: Free $Br_2$ and $I_2$ are non-polar. While they are slightly 
soluble in water, they are highly soluble in non-polar organic solvents like  
$\mathrm{CHCl}_3$ or $\mathrm{CCl}_4$. Since these solvents are denser than water, they form a distinct 
"layer" at the bottom of the test tube.
### Experimental Procedure
To perform the test correctly, follow these steps:

* Acidification: Take a portion of the Sodium Fusion Extract and add dilute $\mathrm{HCl}$ 
or $\mathrm{HNO}_3.$ This neutralizes any NaOH and removes interfering ions like $S^{2-}$ or $CN^-.$

* Solvent Addition: Add a small volume (1-2 mL) of $\mathrm{CHCl}_3$ or $\mathrm{CCl}_4$

* Oxidation: Add chlorine water dropwise and shake the test tube vigorously.

* Settling: Allow the layers to separate and observe the color of the lower
organic layer.
### Observations and reactions
The color of the bottom layer identifies the halogen present:

| Halogen Detected | Chemical Reaction | Color of Organic Layer |
|---|---|---|
| Iodine (I) | $2\mathrm{NaI} + \mathrm{Cl}_2 \rightarrow 2\mathrm{NaCl} + \mathrm{I}_2$ | Violet/Purple |
| Bromine (Br) | $2\mathrm{NaBr} + \mathrm{Cl}_2 \rightarrow 2\mathrm{NaCl} + \mathrm{Br}_2$ | Orange/Brown |
⚠️ Critical Note: If you add excess chlorine water to a violet solution, the
color may disappear. This is because $I_2$ is further oxidized to colorless 
Iodic acid $(\mathrm{HIO}_3)$ :

$\mathrm{I}_2 + 5\mathrm{Cl}_2 + 6\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} \rightarrow 2\mathrm{HIO}_3 + 10\mathrm{HCl}$

![layer-test](Assets/qualitative-analysis/1773710904.png) https://youtu.be/JVXIM8W3g_w?t=53m10s

* $\mathrm{CHCl}_3$ or $\mathrm{CCl}_4$ + $I_2$ or $Br_2$ is the top organic layer (denser) 
* chlorine water + halgen ion is the bottom aqueous layer 

Why is this better than the $\mathrm{AgNO}_3$ test?
While Silver Nitrate ($AgNO_3$) is a great general indicator, its results can be ambiguous:

 * AgBr: Pale yellow precipitate.
 * AgI: Yellow precipitate.
