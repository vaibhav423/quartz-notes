## Thermal stability of carbonates, bicarbonates and nitrates

1. **V. Imp:** Except $Li_2CO_3$, all alkali metal carbonates are thermally stable and do not decompose on heating.

   $$Li_2CO_3(s) \Rightarrow Li_2O(s) + CO_2 \uparrow$$

   $$M_2CO_3(s) \Rightarrow \text{No decomposition}$$
   $[M \rightarrow Na / K / Rb / Cs]$

---

2. Carbonates of alkaline earth metals decompose on heating and liberate $CO_2$ gas.

   $$MCO_3(s) \Rightarrow MO(s) + CO_2 \uparrow$$
   $[M \rightarrow Be / Mg / Ca / Sr / Ba]$

---

3. Following bicarbonates only exist in solid state: 
   $NaHCO_3, KHCO_3, RbHCO_3, CsHCO_3, NH_4HCO_3$

   * Bicarbonates on heating liberate $CO_2$ gas.

   **e.g.** $$2NaHCO_3(s) \Rightarrow Na_2CO_3(s) + H_2O \uparrow + CO_2 \uparrow$$

   **Imp:**
   $$2NaHCO_3(aq) \Rightarrow Na_2CO_3(aq) + H_2O(l) + CO_2 \uparrow$$

   $$Ca(HCO_3)_2(aq) \Rightarrow CaCO_3 \downarrow \text{(white ppt)} + H_2O(l) + CO_2 \uparrow$$

   $$NH_4HCO_3(s) \Rightarrow NH_3 \uparrow + H_2O \uparrow + CO_2 \uparrow$$

---

4. $$MNO_3(s) \Rightarrow MNO_2(s) + \frac{1}{2}O_2 \uparrow$$
   $[M \rightarrow K / Rb / Cs]$

   **e.g.**
   $$KNO_3(s) \Rightarrow KNO_2(s) + O_2 \uparrow$$

   * At $500^\circ C$: 
     $$NaNO_3(s) \Rightarrow NaNO_2(s) + O_2 \uparrow$$
   * At $800^\circ C$: 
     $$NaNO_3 \Rightarrow Na_2O(s) + N_2 \uparrow + O_2 \uparrow$$

   $$LiNO_3(s) \Rightarrow Li_2O(s) + NO_2 \uparrow + O_2 \uparrow$$

---

* $$M(NO_3)_2(s) \Rightarrow MO(s) + NO_2 \uparrow + O_2 \uparrow$$
  $[M \rightarrow Mg / Ca / Sr / Ba / Pb / Cu \text{ etc.}]$

  **e.g.**
  $$Cu(NO_3)_2(s) \Rightarrow CuO(s) + NO_2 \uparrow + O_2 \uparrow$$

  $$Mg(NO_3)_2(s) \Rightarrow MgO(s) + NO_2 \uparrow + O_2 \uparrow \text{ (Unbalanced)}$$

---

### Some other heating effects:

* $$Ag_2O(s) \Rightarrow Ag(s) + O_2 \uparrow$$

* $$HgO(s) \Rightarrow Hg(l) + O_2 \uparrow$$
