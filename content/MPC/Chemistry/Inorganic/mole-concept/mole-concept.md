### Example Calculation: Determining the Composition of 109% Oleum

To understand what "109% Oleum" means, we calculate the amount of free sulfur
trioxide ($SO_3$) present in 100 grams of the mixture.

#### 1. Given Information
- **Label**: 109% Oleum
- **Total Mass of Oleum**: $100\text{ g}$
- **Theoretical Mass of $H_2SO_4$ after hydration**: $109\text{ g}$

#### 2. Mass of Water Needed
The difference between the theoretical mass of sulfuric acid and the
original mass of oleum is the mass of water ($H_2O$) required to react with
all the free $SO_3$:
$$\text{Mass of } H_2O = 109\text{ g} - 100\text{ g} = 9\text{ g}$$

#### 3. Stoichiometry of the Hydration Reaction
The reaction is:
$$SO_3 + H_2O \rightarrow H_2SO_4$$
Molar masses:
- $SO_3 \approx 80\text{ g/mol}$
- $H_2O \approx 18\text{ g/mol}$

From the equation, $18\text{ g}$ of $H_2O$ reacts with $80\text{ g}$ of $SO_3$. Therefore, $9\text{ g}$ of $H_2O$ will react with:
$$\text{Mass of free } SO_3 = \frac{80}{18} \times 9\text{ g} = 40\text{ g}$$

#### 4. Final Composition of 100g of 109% Oleum
- **Free $SO_3$**: $40\text{ g}$ (or 40%)
- **Pure $H_2SO_4$**: $100\text{ g} - 40\text{ g} = 60\text{ g}$ (or 60%)

**Conclusion**: 109% oleum consists of 40% free $SO_3$ dissolved in 60% pure $H_2SO_4$.
