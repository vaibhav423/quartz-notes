## problem (dalton rlvp)

Two liquids A and B form an ideal solution at temperature T K . At T K , the vapour pressures of pure A and B are 55 and $15 \mathrm{kN} \mathrm{m}^{-2}$ respectively. What is the mole fraction of A in solution of A and B in equilibrium with a vapour in which the mole fraction of A is 0.8?

**`sol`:**



$ P = p_A+p_B = 55x_A+15(1-x_A) = 55x_A+15-15x_A = 40x_A+15 $


$ y_A = \frac{p_A}{P} = \frac{55x_A}{40x_A+15} = 0.8 $

## problem (molality tough) 

Sea water, which can be considered as a 6 molar $(6 \mathrm{M})$ solution of NaCl , has a
density of $2 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mL}^{-1}$. The concentration of dissolved oxygen $\left(\mathrm{O}_2\right)$ in sea water 
is 5.8 ppm . Then the concentration of dissolved oxygen $\left(\mathrm{O}_2\right)$ in sea water, is $x \times 10^{-4} \mathrm{~m}$.

**`sol`:**

$\text{Mass of solution} = \text{Volume} \times \text{Density} = 1000\text{ mL} \times 2\text{ g/mL} = 2000\text{ g}$

$\text{Mass of NaCl} = 6\text{ mol} \times 58.5\text{ g/mol} = 351\text{ g}$

$\text{ppm} = \frac{\text{Mass of solute}}{\text{Mass of solution}} \times 10^6$

$\text{Mass of solvent} = \text{Total mass} - (\text{Mass of NaCl} + \text{Mass of } O_2)$

$\text{Molality } (m) = \frac{\text{Moles of solute}}{\text{Mass of solvent in kg}}$
